# Ship the agent loop, shelve the launcher

Upgrade should spend the next cycle closing the gap between "watch agents over SSH" and "steer them": send follow-up prompts from the conversation view, offer QR pairing, ship a free open-source APK outside Google Play under a chosen license, and defer the Android Home mode and per-window Linux GUI that the current strategy treats as required. The October 2026 evidence moved in two directions at once. The phone-remote layer was commoditized: Anthropic, OpenAI, Cursor and GitHub all ship phone control of their own agents, each tied to an account and a vendor relay, and every one of them lets the user send a follow-up, which Upgrade does not. Meanwhile the launcher and on-phone Linux desktop ground was taken by OmaLauncher, which shipped 18 releases in 18 days with a real default launcher and Wayland windows as separate Android tasks. That leaves one position no product holds: **a free, open-source, account-free, relay-free Android client that reaches any SSH host, multiplexes many agents from many vendors, and can also run them on the phone**. Moshi comes closest and now ships on Android with herdr, Mosh and QR pairing, but it is closed and paid. Paseo is open and account-free but needs its own daemon. The strategy's "programmable Home across computers" idea survives as an in-app and widget composition model. The plan to win daily-launcher parity does not, because Upgrade would enter late against a competitor that ships daily, on a platform that penalizes third-party launchers.

## Every major vendor now steers agents from the phone, and Upgrade cannot yet send a prompt

The ROADMAP's competitor table was written before the vendors finished moving. **Claude Code Remote Control** connects the Claude iOS and Android apps to a session on the user's own machine, syncs messages across terminal, browser and phone, and pushes "when actions required". It requires a Pro, Max, Ultra, Team or Enterprise subscription (no API keys), and Anthropic's servers route every message between phone and machine ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/remote-control)). **Codex in the ChatGPT app** lets the phone start or continue chats, send follow-ups, approve commands and review diffs. Its hosts must be the ChatGPT desktop app on macOS or Windows, and Linux devboxes are reachable only through SSH from that desktop ([ChatGPT docs](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/remote-connections)). Users report that `codex remote-control` on a Linux CLI fails to connect ([openai/codex #9200](https://github.com/openai/codex/discussions/9200)). Cursor's native app is **iPhone and iPad only, with Android "planned"** ([Cursor docs](https://cursor.com/docs/cloud-agent/mobile)), and GitHub Mobile assigns agents to issues in GitHub's cloud ([GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-04-01-github-mobile-faster-more-flexible-agent-assignment-from-issues/)).

The open-source field consolidated into a few multi-agent clients with native Android apps. **Happy** has 24.4k stars and relays end-to-end-encrypted sync through happy-server ([GitHub](https://github.com/slopus/happy)). **Paseo** has 22.6k stars, Apache-2.0, native Android, a local daemon reached over direct TCP, Tailscale or an optional relay, and "no telemetry, tracking, or forced log-ins" ([GitHub](https://github.com/getpaseo/paseo)). **HAPI** (5.3k stars, AGPL) charges for its native apps ([GitHub](https://github.com/tiann/hapi)). Churn is high: Omnara pivoted away from its phone app after 10K+ Play downloads, and Terragon and Crystal shut down ([Omnara Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.omnara.app&hl=en), [terragon-oss](https://github.com/terragon-labs/terragon-oss)). Single-vendor remotes lost their reason to exist once vendors shipped their own.

Across this whole field the common pattern is push, then approve, then type or dictate a follow-up. **Every competitor whose docs cover it sends follow-up prompts as structured input**: Claude, Codex, Cursor, Happy, Happier, Paseo and amux (HAPI and Orca do not say) ([comparison sources](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/remote-control), [Paseo](https://github.com/getpaseo/paseo), [Happier](https://github.com/happier-dev/happier)). Upgrade's SPEC deliberately routes the user to the agent's shell pane instead, and ROADMAP schedules "compose follow-ups" at R2. Upgrade already has most of what this needs: a terminal composer and dictation for agent tabs, and herdr pane addressing that names the target pane. The missing piece is delivery from the conversation view into that pane, with a visible result. This is the single largest functional gap against every app a prospective user will compare Upgrade with, and it should move into the release target.

Two other vendor weaknesses are Upgrade strengths that are not yet proven. Claude's docs warn that **Android battery optimization delays pushes** ([docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/remote-control)). A reviewer complained that Omnara raised no Android notifications ([Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.omnara.app&hl=en)). Claude Remote Control on Android drops and never auto-reconnects ([anthropics/claude-code #34255](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/34255)). Upgrade's watcher already posts its own notifications from a foreground service and answers approvals from the notification, with no relay (SPEC "The ongoing notification says what the agents are"). The connection supervisor exists to reconnect. Neither has been measured on the release build (ROADMAP Stage 3 remains open). If they hold up under test, they are the claims to lead with.

## Moshi now covers Upgrade's shell feature list on Android; free and open is what remains

The ROADMAP frames Moshi's advantage as "first connection". That is now too narrow. Moshi has been on Google Play since May 2026, with **10k+ downloads and a 4.75 rating from 279 ratings** at v4.0.2. Its listing title is now "Moshi: tmux/herdr SSH terminal" ([AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/moshi-tmux-herdr-ssh-terminal/app.getmoshi.android), [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.getmoshi.android&hl=en_US)). It speaks SSH, Mosh and ET with "no session relay". It integrates herdr, tmux and Zellij, and offers on-device voice-to-terminal, image paste and annotation, and OSC 52 clipboard ([getmoshi.app](https://getmoshi.app)). A host-side `moshi-hook` adds an agent kanban, an approve/deny inbox, a diff viewer and usage tracking for 18 agents ([install-moshi-hook](https://getmoshi.app/docs/install-moshi-hook)). **Easy Pair** has a host command print a QR code; the phone generates the key and the host installs the public key ([Moshi install docs](https://getmoshi.app/docs/install)). Its developer has said it uses libghostty on iOS and will on Android ([Hashimoto thread](https://x.com/mitchellh/status/2025601387258941743?lang=en)). Feature by feature, the overlap with Upgrade is close to one-to-one.

Upgrade can still differ on terms, not features. Moshi is closed source and freemium: **Pro costs $9.99/mo, $89.99/yr or $199 lifetime**. Its pricing page lists Mosh/ET and tmux/herdr auto-attach as Pro features, while the free tier keeps push and usage tracking ([Moshi pricing](https://getmoshi.app/pricing)). The detailed free/Pro split page was not fetched, so treat the exact gating as provisional. A free Upgrade with Mosh, herdr, a cross-host approval inbox, a code view, VNC and phone Linux undercuts that directly. Android has room for it. **JuiceSSH was unpublished from Play in December 2025**, and its paid features vanished because its license checks depended on a server ([Eric Park](https://ericswpark.com/blog/2026/2026-06-03-juicessh/)). ConnectBot was revived in Kotlin/Compose and is adding Mosh through a separate GPL binary ([connectbot.org](https://connectbot.org/), [PR #2169](https://github.com/connectbot/connectbot/pull/2169)). Termius moved its Android terminal to libghostty on 31 October 2026, offers Mosh free, and has no agent features ([APKMirror 8.0.0](https://www.apkmirror.com/apk/termius-corporation/termius-ssh-telnet-client/termius-modern-ssh-client-8-0-0-release/), [Termius pricing](https://termius.com/pricing)). Rendering quality is now table stakes, not a moat. Flutter bindings to libghostty already exist ([awesome-libghostty](https://github.com/Uzaaft/awesome-libghostty/blob/master/README.md)), so flterm is not a selling point. The workflow is.

QR pairing is no longer optional polish. ROADMAP Stage 2 lists "QR pairing that installs a key" as an undecided trust question. Moshi ships it and Claude uses QR handoff ([Claude mobile docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/mobile)), so users now expect it. Moshi's design also fits Upgrade's rule that the app never writes `authorized_keys` itself: **the host-side command installs the key**. Upgrade can go further than Moshi by putting the host-key fingerprint in the QR code, so the first connection is pinned rather than trusted on first use. That is an inference from the design, not a tested feature.

## OmaLauncher took the launcher and Linux-window ground the strategy calls required

STRATEGY says Upgrade "has to meet" Oma's breadth and keeps Android Home (W5) and local GUI windows (W8) as "required ambitions". The October evidence argues against spending scarce effort there. OmaLauncher's repo was created on 2 October 2026. By 31 October it had **504 stars, 109 forks and 18 GitHub releases from v0.5.0 to v0.16.0**, ships on Google Play, and is MIT-licensed while bundling GPL proot ([GitHub](https://github.com/SimonSchubert/omalauncher), [releases](https://github.com/SimonSchubert/omalauncher/releases)). It *is* the Wayland compositor for a proot Arch install: "every Linux window opens as its own Android task, with its own card in Recents". It also has GPU acceleration on Mali and Adreno 6xx+ and Omarchy themes ([README](https://github.com/SimonSchubert/omalauncher)). Since 20 October alone it has added:

- an agent-editable key bar and home layout
- a control center with media, DND and notifications
- keyboard and gamepad navigation
- store updates that do not overwrite user- or agent-edited widgets
- multi-page "Faces"
- WebSockets and pinned certificates in widgets

([releases](https://github.com/SimonSchubert/omalauncher/releases)). Upgrade's W8 feasibility study would be starting where Oma already ships.

The platform also taxes any third-party launcher. **Xiaomi HyperOS forces 3-button navigation when a third-party launcher is the default**, and Pixel still gives non-Quickstep launchers weaker gesture and recents behavior. OmaLauncher's maintainer closed an accessibility-service workaround because it needed `WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS` and `KILL_BACKGROUND_PROCESSES` ([OmaLauncher #19](https://github.com/SimonSchubert/omalauncher/issues/19), [XDA](https://xdaforums.com/t/third-party-system-launchers-return-to-home-screen-delay-gesture-navigation-only.4655464/page-2)). Private space adds mandatory launcher obligations: a separate container, lock and unlock, and hiding its apps from search ([Android 15 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-all)). A Flutter launcher would have to build all of these over platform channels before it reached parity.

On per-window Linux GUI, Google's AVF Terminal draws every app inside one Weston window. It runs on Pixel/Tensor and some Exynos and MediaTek chips, but **not on Snapdragon flagships**, because they lack non-protected VM support ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/snapdragon-chips-android-linux-terminal-3608648/), [GUI hands-on](https://www.androidauthority.com/linux-terminal-graphical-apps-3580905/)). Per-window prototypes already exist and could be reused if the work is ever picked up: lindroids (MIT/Apache, one APK per app), tawc (GPL-3.0, libhybris GPU) and MagicDesk (GPLv3, Shizuku "Shroot") ([lindroids](https://github.com/dixonSolutions/lindroids), [tawc](https://github.com/wmww/tawc), [MagicDesk](https://github.com/LilYoopug/magicdesk)).

Two of Oma's choices leave room for Upgrade. First, its review notes that **"proot is not a sandbox"**: agents see everything the launcher can access, and a YOLO setting skips APK-install approval ([opportunist.global](https://opportunist.global/index.php/2026/10/17/omalauncher-ai-android-launcher-linux-coding-agents/)). Upgrade's Luau cards run in a VM behind declared capabilities. That sits between Oma's full access and the platform generators, Google's Create My Widget and Nothing's Playground, which make small, tightly permissioned widgets and hide their code ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/12/google-brings-agentic-ai-and-vibe-coded-widgets-to-android/), [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/02/10/nothing-essential-apps-hands-on/)). Second, Oma's agents run on the phone, while Upgrade's main path reaches real compute over SSH. The defensible version of "programmable Home" is therefore **cards that act on remote machines, shown inside Upgrade and as launcher widgets on the user's existing launcher**. That is STRATEGY's third renderer, and it avoids the gesture tax entirely.

There is also a concrete first card no competitor occupies. ServerCat reads CPU, RAM and disk over SSH without an agent, but it is iOS-only ([swiftserver.app](https://swiftserver.app/blog/best-server-monitoring-app-iphone)). NixOS deployers such as comin have **no UI of their own**; users build Grafana dashboards on top ([comin](https://github.com/nlewo/comin)). Home Assistant's card dashboards are the right model for many machines on one phone screen ([CloudApp.dev](https://www.cloudapp.dev/home-assistant-custom-cards-mushroom-button-card)). A host card showing liveness, load and agent state over plain SSH, plus the NixOS generation through the Engine, is exactly STRATEGY's W2/W3 "first native host/resource contribution".

The plugin ecosystem evidence also amends the catalog plan. Raycast grew to roughly 2,000 extensions with mandatory open source and PR-based review ([Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/raycast-vs-alfred-2026/)). In May 2026 Obsidian dropped manual review for **automated per-version scanning with a public scorecard**. The trigger was coding agents flooding its queue: the new system cleared 2,300+ queued submissions in its first days ([Obsidian blog](https://obsidian.md/blog/future-of-plugins/)). Its plugin count roughly tripled in six months, much of it "vibe-coded abandonware" ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/obsidian-has-a-plugin-problem/)). Nothing paused publishing to Playground ([nothing.wiki](https://nothing.wiki/software/essential_apps_and_playground)). Upgrade's author is "a user working with a coding agent", so its catalog will hit the same flood. The existing `check` tool and capability manifests should be the automated gate, with human review reserved for featured packages. Luau has real embedded use (Alan Wake 2, Warframe), but no mobile scripting product uses it ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45289558)), so Upgrade's plugin system is novel but has no ecosystem to borrow. MCP on phones is cloud-brokered and cannot reach local servers ([Claude support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11503834-building-custom-connectors-via-remote-mcp-servers)). An MCP endpoint on the Engine is a reasonable later interop step. A WebView renderer for MCP Apps would weaken the sandbox story and is not needed.

## Play policy and 2027 verification decide the channel; Mosh provenance decides the license

Phone Linux is incompatible with Google Play as written. Play bans downloading "executable code (such as dex, JAR, .so files) from a source other than Google Play", with an exception for code running in a VM or interpreter ([Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16273414)). Luau plausibly fits that exception; downloaded rootfs binaries do not. Termux's Play build now runs downloaded binaries at targetSdk 37 through `/system/bin/linker64` plus an APK-bundled PRoot loader, and **its maintainers say this route "does not comply with Play's executable-code policy" and expect Android to close it** ([sparkles PR #653](https://github.com/PetarKirov/sparkles/pull/653)). That is the largest platform risk to Upgrade's phone host, which uses the same mechanism. Two more constraints apply. Play requires targetSdk 36 for updates since 31 August 2026 ([Play target API](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11926878?hl=en)), and it **blocks updates without 16 KB page-size support from 1 February 2027** ([Android page sizes](https://developer.android.com/guide/practices/page-sizes)). PRoot's loader and mmap assumptions on 16 KB kernels are unverified. Upgrade's manifest already declares `specialUse` and `dataSync` foreground services, and `specialUse` requires a justification that Play reviews ([FGS types](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/fgs-types-required)).

Off-Play distribution works today and has a known cost later. Developer verification has applied since 30 September 2026 only to installs from participating stores in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand. **Direct sideloads are not covered "yet", and global enforcement is set for 2027** with no exact date. ADB installs are exempt, and a Full Distribution account in the Android Developer Console costs $25 ([FAQ](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq), [Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification)). Termux's mainline has shipped via F-Droid and GitHub since 2020. IzzyOnDroid ships developer-signed APKs and pins their certificates, and Obtainium installs straight from release pages ([Termux wiki](https://github.com/termux/termux-packages/wiki/Termux-and-Android-10), [IzzyOnDroid review](https://privacygear.nl/en/reviews/izzyon-droid-review/)). F-Droid main re-signs most apps and calls verification "existential" ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/f-droid-says-googles-android-developer-verification-plan-is-an-existential-threat-to-alternative-app-stores/)), so it is the most exposed channel. Developer-signed APKs on GitHub Releases, listed on IzzyOnDroid and Obtainium, with the signing key registered in the Developer Console before 2027, is the lowest-risk route. OmaLauncher and Andronix being on Play shows that PRoot apps can get listed. It does not show they are safe from removal, and Termux's maintainer warns of "sudden" removal ([termux-app #2155](https://github.com/termux/termux-app/issues/2155)).

The `dart_mosh` question is settled. Upgrade depends on `dart_mosh` 0.0.4 from pub.dev (github.com/gwitko/dart_mosh), patched under `.dart_tool`, and the repository has no root `LICENSE`. Mosh itself is **GPLv3 with OpenSSL and iOS exceptions** ([mosh.org](https://mosh.org/)), but the package ships under **Apache-2.0** and its NOTICE states it "is a clean-room Dart implementation of the Mosh client protocol. It is not derived from and does not incorporate source from the upstream mosh project", the same route Termius took ([Wikipedia: Mosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosh_(software))). Upgrade is therefore free to choose its license; Upgrade's own patches to the package must keep the Apache NOTICE. GPLv3 remains a reasonable choice on its merits: it fits the peers and channels that matter: OpenMinis, Kvaesitso, F-Droid and IzzyOnDroid ([F-Droid Kvaesitso](https://f-droid.org/en/packages/de.mm20.launcher2.release/)). Its main cost is an iOS App Store build, which the ROADMAP already excludes, and libghostty's MIT license imposes nothing. Whatever the app license, the plugin SDK, templates and reference packages should be permissive (MIT) so authors are not bound by the app's terms. On money: JuiceSSH's server-checked licensing stranded paying users, Nova's ads caused a backlash, and Niagara's subscription draws "basic features" complaints ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/1/nova-launcher-is-being-revived-with-a-new-owner-but-you-will-now-see-ads-unless-you-pay), [Niagara Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=bitpit.launcher&hl=en_US)). A local-first app with no backend has nothing that justifies a subscription. Free, with donations or sponsorship, is consistent with the product and is the clearest contrast with Moshi.

| 11 | Describe device screens as monitoring until a scrcpy-server path over the existing adb tooling exists | Positioning | scrcpy-class apps reach 35–70 ms with no PC |
| 12 | Do not build a relay, accounts, a subscription, an MCP Apps WebView renderer or an iOS port; consider an Engine MCP endpoint later | Stop | Vendor relays are the thing to contrast against; JuiceSSH and Nova; Claude mobile MCP is cloud-only |

Two uncertainties carry real weight in this plan. No source gives Android install numbers for Happy, Paseo or HAPI, so the size of the "Android, no relay" audience is inferred from star counts and roundups rather than usage. Stack Overflow's 2025 survey does show Android as the most-used personal OS among developers, at 29% ([SO 2025](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/technology)). And whether Google will ever enforce the executable-code policy against PRoot apps, or close linker64 exec, is unknown in either direction.

## Conclusion

The competitive picture splits Upgrade's own documents. ROADMAP's agent-workbench thesis is stronger than when it was written: vendors validated demand, then limited themselves to one vendor, an account and a relay each, and Android users on Snapdragon phones and Linux hosts remain underserved. STRATEGY's launcher-and-desktop expansion is weaker: the one competitor built for it ships daily, and Android penalizes third-party launchers in ways a Flutter app cannot route around. The strategic unit is the remote resource on a card, not the launcher grid.

What decides Upgrade's position is one feature and one channel decision, not breadth. With follow-up delivery, QR pairing and a license in place, Upgrade becomes the only free, open, relay-free Android client that steers many agents on many machines and can also run them on the phone. Without them, it competes feature-for-feature with Moshi on Moshi's terms, and with Oma on Oma's.
